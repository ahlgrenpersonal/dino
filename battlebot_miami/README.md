# Battle Bot Miami

A Miami chapter using the standard Bot Adventure's robot and equipment artwork, workshop layout, equipment definitions, Power control, atlas controls, styles, audio controls and camera rules. It installs separately and keeps its own progress. Miami reward weapons have their own bubble-wand, bat-wing, conga-mallet, branching-staff, surf-spinner, speaker-rocket and royal-crystal silhouettes, shared between menus and equipped bots. The map, landmarks, staged chapters and monster bosses belong to Miami.

Tap land to walk and the bot automatically battles nearby monsters. Tap a monster for Adventure's stronger targeted strike; the lightning button gives the familiar six-second Power boost, ready every 28 seconds. Three battery pictures wake each district boss. Boss portraits, weapons, arrows and check marks carry the play flow without requiring reading. Startup protection is a steady shield.

Build walks back to Panorama's free workshop. The original four tabs are Weapons, Armor, Wheels and Gear, with six colors, vertical equipment rows and separate build/equip buttons. Start with the Bumper alone; weapons appear only after being earned, one matching weapon per boss, with no weapon crafting. Existing earned equipment remains available. Only owned equipment and currently affordable, unlocked upgrades appear. Craft non-weapon upgrades with scrap and parts; Bubble Shield, Aegis Shield, armor, wheel types, magnets, Charge Coupler, Power Capacitor and Helper Drone have their familiar effects. Weapon names and descriptions accompany large, distinct equipment pictures. The workshop’s ⓘ toggle controls extra statistics. The world and map always use pictures without place-name captions, including older saves; progression guidance stays inside Map. Existing Miami weapons, currency, upgrades and discoveries migrate into this equipment system.

The map uses Adventure's pinch/pan, zoom buttons, fit button and highlighted current destination; other destinations stay collapsed. Public playgrounds have green-ring charging pads: the ＋ button spends two scrap to recharge, and Charge Coupler doubles the rate. Safe playgrounds prevent both damage and attacks. Gentle mode starts on; rescues retain discoveries and equipment.

Tap ♪ to enable the original Miami instrumental: a sunny Latin-inspired exploration theme, about 95 seconds long, with a smooth loop and balanced background level. It uses Adventure's audio controls, keeps playing through menus, and pauses when muted or the page is hidden. Offline byte-range playback is supported. Browser audio requires an initial gesture.

Taps on water, private property or locked areas quietly stop movement. The map also ignores inaccessible destinations without a warning sign.

Brickell and Brickell Key form one opening chapter. Easy vampires wait outside Panorama Tower’s 3.25-unit safe radius, with two mainland battery camps and one on the Key. Count Neon’s easy first boss fight on the Key unlocks Little Havana. City Centre offers Trail Tires; 1814 Brickell Park offers Steel Armor. Domino Park, Regatta Park, Crandon Beach, Bayside and Museum Park offer useful gear, armor or wheels. Finds are permanent and do not replace the selected weapon. Brickell Key is a waterfront circuit around an inaccessible residential center. Little Havana has Domino Park, Tower Theater and a riverside playground, with mummy robots to battle.

Coconut Grove includes CocoWalk, Peacock Park, Park Grove’s rounded towers, Grove at Grand Bay’s twisting towers, the curved balconies of Mr. C Residences, Regatta Park, City Hall and Dinner Key Marina. Mechanical peacocks with blue necks, eye-spotted fans and wheels join the witch robots in combat.

Downtown’s waterfront has separate pictures for Bayfront Park and its pirate-ship playground, the circular jet fountain, Bayside Marketplace, Miamarina’s boats and dining terraces, the observation wheel, the arena and Museum Park. Frost Science has the planetarium dome; the nearby art museum is a quieter backdrop.

Rickenbacker Causeway reaches Hobie Beach and Virginia Key. North Point Beach and Crandon Beach have lifeguard-tower illustrations. Bear Cut Bridge leads to Key Biscayne; Cape Florida’s lighthouse and beach are farther south than Crandon. Captain Rattle waits near Cape Florida. The Frost Queen opens MacArthur Causeway to Miami Beach. After the beach camps and every earlier boss, the King near South Pointe awards the Fire-Jet Launcher.

The camera is ten percent farther out than the preceding Miami build. The Queen and King have a visible cyan shield with Power and tap pictograms. Ordinary damage can chip them to a quarter health; landing a direct targeted strike fired during Power breaks the shield for the rest of that fight. Armor and gear keep these active battles forgiving. Other bosses encourage Power without a shield requirement.

Each chapter appears only when unlocked. After victory, all locations stay visible. Respawning restores every camp and boss while preserving equipment, parts, scrap, stamps and color. Clear the other bosses again to challenge the King in each new round. Old saves keep earned progress when the map expands. The current unfinished chapter and its next battery or boss are marked yellow, including each new replay round; already unlocked geography stays visible.

The game preserves broad relative locations with an enlarged arcade scale. It does not track real-world beach conditions, lifeguard hours or temporary access changes. The Cape Florida state-park beach has no lifeguard-tower illustration.

From the repository root, run `npm start --prefix battlebot_miami`, then open `http://127.0.0.1:4217/`. No dependency installation is needed. `MIAMI_PORT` selects another port. `npm test --prefix battlebot_miami` checks campaign completion, landmark walks, private boundaries, bridge-only crossings, combat, peacocks, rewards, replay, saves, picture rendering, viewport changes and PWA behavior.

Keyboard: arrows/WASD move, Space gives Power, M opens the map, and P/Escape pauses. Native keyboard controls work within paused dialogs. The PWA caches only listed app assets, uses separate save/cache namespaces, and never loads personal place overrides. Build world16.
