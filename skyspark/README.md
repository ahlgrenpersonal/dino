# SkySpark

A touch-first, invincible superhero flying game. All scenery and characters are drawn by Canvas; music is synthesized locally. No photos, external fonts, analytics or runtime inference.

The eight worlds are each 28,800 units wide, with 54 spatial robot encounters and smoothly repeating scenery. Sunbeam City ends with Iron Colossus (24 armor), Emerald Plains with the gigantic bucket-wheel Earthmover (36), and Stormpeak Range with Stormwing (48). Ember Caldera ends with Magma Bastion (64 armor), and Moonlit Spaceport ends with Orbit Dreadnought (84). Sandglass Dunes has the Scarab Siege Walker (90), Neon Reef has Abyss Leviathan (100), and Prism Citadel ends with Chrome Echo, an angular robotic superhero rival fought in three rounds (40 / 40 / 120 health). Ordinary robot totals are 864 / 1,080 / 1,620 / 1,620 / 1,944 / 1,944 / 1,944 / 1,944, plus eight boss defenders per world. Only nearby encounters stay active. Bosses appear at the far end independently of skipped enemies.

Sparks jumps consecutively through up to 20 visible targets, reaching 760 units initially and 360 between jumps; recharge is 1 second. Laser sweeps up to 75 degrees, pierces targets for 5 damage each, and recharges in 2 seconds. Big Blast expands to radius 562.5, deals 1 damage and recharges in 2.2 seconds. Sparks damage is 0.8; flying punches deal 1. A punch counts once on a deliberate entry; retreat at least 110 units clear of the silhouette before another punch. Hovering, enemy movement and knockback do not punch, and there is no passive proximity damage. Every weapon clears incoming fire. All enemy damage checks current viewport visibility, including delayed effects; chains cannot carry damage off-screen.

Enemy shots hit the hero directly. He never loses health or progress, but gets a short directional push: roughly 18 units for plasma, 30 for missiles, 38 for rocks and 28 for beams. Push velocity decays quickly, is bounded, respects world edges and never disables direct movement. There is no automatic shield bubble. The mining boss carries rocks around its wheel, winds up visibly and throws three slow ballistic boulders. Flying punches and all powers shatter rocks. Leaving view during wind-up cancels that throw.

Tap the sky to fly, a rooftop to land or a robot to smash. Keyboard: arrows/WASD, X Sparks, L Laser, Space Blast, M worlds, Escape pause. The illustrated world chooser uses colorful cards, locks and a recent-completion trophy. Boss defeat celebrates for three foreground seconds before opening it. Completed worlds replay with one tap; unfinished flights resume independently. Pause and backgrounding freeze play.

Schema 5 migrates old progress to the expanded worlds once, preserving relative position, unlocks, defeated regions, flight tally, sound preference and proportional boss damage. Manual restart keeps a recoverable backup. Saves stay on the device.

The last three worlds keep most robots easy to shatter. Rare ground soldiers have three health, larger armored bodies and slow heavy plasma shots. The laser still defeats them in one hit. These soldiers push roughly 140 units; Scarab attacks push 200 and Leviathan attacks 300. Chrome Echo appears around 5,600 units, returns around 15,000, and fights to the finish at the end. The first two 40-health encounters end with a stationary teleport effect, no defeat tally and no level victory. The final encounter has 120 health and ends with an explosion or a laser split. Damage and phase persist across reloads; skipped encounters catch up without forcing backtracking. Chrome Echo alternates an orange laser, magenta lightning and an orange expanding blast. Its laser warns for 1.3 seconds with fixed aim, then can push 650–1,100 world units depending on viewport width. Lightning pushes 180 and the blast 400. All are clearable with all three hero powers. Strong recoil decays over a brief recovery period, respects world bounds, never changes facing and never disables flight. Each new boss has regular shield openings; Big Blast interrupts its shield for two seconds. Older five-world saves append three fresh runs, unlocking the sixth world only after completing the fifth.

Serve over localhost or HTTPS. Install with Safari Share > Add to Home Screen or a supported browser install menu. The isolated service worker supports offline play and updates without clearing local progress. Build: world110.

Run npm test for campaigns, weapons, viewport protection, world expansion/migration, impacts and escape controls, mining boss fights, incoming-fire defense, world chooser and audio recovery. node tools/verify-workers.mjs <release-root> additionally checks shared-origin PWA isolation. Browser checks do not replace a physical iPhone Home Screen playtest.

Worlds four and five mix in visibly plated robots with 2 health: one punch, blast or Sparks hit leaves them alive, while a five-damage laser defeats them immediately. New bosses fire larger, slow fans of projectiles and cause stronger but capped pushes. Magma Bastion raises its shield for three seconds out of six; the Dreadnought raises it for four. Shields reduce incoming damage to 45%, never zero. Big Blast breaks them for two seconds. Their exposed hulls and claws remain hittable only while visible. Old three-world saves keep all unlocks and add the two new runs without resetting progress.
